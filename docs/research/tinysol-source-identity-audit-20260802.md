# TinySOL v6.0 source-identity audit

Date: 2026-08-02

State: source-identity evidence only; no source allocation or factor freeze

## Outcome

The official TinySOL v6.0 audio archive and metadata reconcile exactly after
accounting for the archive's declared `./` root prefix and six bound
`.DS_Store` members. The metadata has 2,913 unique WAV paths, and the archive
has exactly those 2,913 audio members. Every PCM payload is distinct.

The 2,273 rows whose filename MISC field is `N` and whose metadata says no
digital retuning are eligible to become paired encoder masters. The other 640
rows are known PCM transformations: 600 tuning-only, 28 semitone-resampled,
and 12 both tuned and resampled. All 40 `R` rows resolve to exactly one parent
path; 39 distinct parents are involved.

TinySOL still contributes one conservative partition group, not 14 instrument
groups or 2,913 recordings. Its metadata does not identify performers,
physical instruments, microphones, or per-file sessions, and every row shares
the related SOL recording and processing chain. The proposed encoder-transfer
population therefore remains 172 groups across four domains, 72 above its
minimum of 100.

## Provider claims versus acquired bytes

The [official v6 record](https://zenodo.org/records/3685367) describes 2,913
ordinary-style notes from 14 instruments, recorded for Studio On Line at Ircam
in the late 1990s. It describes mono 44.1 kHz, 16-bit WAVs and explains the
filename's natural (`N`), tuning (`T`), and missing-pitch resampling (`R`)
markers. The acquired archive confirms the audio count and representation.

Four provider-description details are not archive invariants:

- the record says the metadata has 13 columns, but it enumerates and
  distributes 14;
- it says Instance ID ranges from 0 to 4, but three accordion rows use 5 and
  correctly encode `alt5`;
- it says clips last 2 to 10 seconds, while the archive spans about 1.315 to
  16.235 seconds; and
- the metadata paths omit the tar's `./` prefix and six `.DS_Store` members.

These are recorded discrepancies, not reasons to repair provider data. The
audit strips exactly one bound `./` archive prefix for comparison, binds the
six ancillary members by path, size, and SHA-256, and rejects any other
unexpected or special member.

## Provenance and processing boundary

The related primary
[OrchideaSOL paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2007.00763) says TinySOL,
OrchideaSOL, and FullSOL were processed in the same recovery programme. It
reports that the original SOL sessions used six channels at 48 kHz and 24-bit:
a stereo reference pair, a proximity microphone, an internal microphone, and
two distant bidirectional microphones. The derivatives were cleaned and
trimmed, resampled to 44.1 kHz, and made mono by retaining the proximity
channel. The programme also applied volume compensation and a global negative
make-up gain, while avoiding nonlinear limiting and compression.

Some notes then received digital pitch correction, and missing pitches were
generated by resampling nearby notes. The bound TinySOL archive adds a current
16-bit distribution boundary. No lossy coding stage is documented, so the
files remain suitable Tier A no-lossy-history challenges. They are not raw or
untouched microphone masters.

This distinction is experimentally useful. Every TinySOL reference carries
common sample-rate conversion, channel extraction, trimming, gain, and
bit-depth confounds. The `N` marker means only that no additional per-note
retuning is declared. A mechanism that responds to these PCM operations as if
they were codec history must fail the hard-negative analysis.

## Grouping and transform decision

Each eligible non-retuned distributed WAV may define one `source_group` for
its reference and controlled codec derivatives. All TinySOL source groups
remain inside one `partition_group` and one provider stratum because the
recording and processing dependencies are not separable from public metadata.
The paper's two broad SOL recording phases do not establish independent
performers, hardware, or processing lineages for these files.

The 40 `R` rows have a deterministic metadata parent: reverse the documented
100-cent direction while holding instrument, dynamics, instance, and any
prior `T` adjustment fixed. Every `R` row must inherit that parent's
`source_group`. Twelve of those parents are themselves tuning-only; the other
28 are `N` rows. Tuning-only rows have no distributed pre-retuning PCM and may
not be represented as paired before/after transforms.

Digitally retuned rows are not base encoder masters. They remain declared
PCM-transform candidates for the later source/factor freeze, where their
lineage, weighting, and assessment role must be fixed before mechanism scores
are inspected.

## Storage and next gate

After retaining TinySOL, the data volume had about 37.79 GiB free. The
remaining planned archives total about 19.20 GiB. Preserving the 15 GiB reserve
would leave only about 3.59 GiB for bounded references, partials, and derived
cases. The reserve still holds, but the acquisition order must remain one
low-priority worker with streaming audits and a fresh free-space projection
before each source.

The remaining source gate is VCTK, Speech Commands, and RWC audio identity
verification. Only after those archives are bound may source allocation,
factor levels, and toolchain versions be frozen separately.

No benchmark derivative, mechanism score, candidate decision, or transfer
score was opened by this audit.
